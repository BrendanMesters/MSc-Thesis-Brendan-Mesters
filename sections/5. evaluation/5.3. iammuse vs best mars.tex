\label{section: evaluation - iammuse vs best mars}

The final two MARS models considered are \textit{MARS partial standard} and \textit{MARS standard to free}.
As discussed in \cref{section: experimental setup - baseline analysis}, these models are trained using a subset of the \textit{interpretation data}, the rest of which is used for testing.
These models will assess the accuracy of the MARS model when it is trained with highly representative data, representing a \textit{best-case scenario} for the MARS architecture.
Furthermore, the training set size for these models, 18,531 and 25,139 frames, respectively, is comparable to the 24,066-frame training set utilised by the MARS paper.

The accuracy of \textit{MARS partial standard} and \textit{MARS standard to free} is significantly higher than the previously considered MARS models, as shown in \cref{figure: best mars arm accuracy}.
Prior models had an accuracy below 0.4 for individual \textit{arm position} predictions, and an accuracy below 0.2 for \textit{pose state} predictions.
\textit{MARS partial standard} and \textit{standard to free} however, both have an accuracy above 0.65 for the \textit{arm position} predictions, and above 0.45 for the \textit{pose state} prediction.
Even with this increase in accuracy, however, IAmMuse still outperforms these models by a significant margin.

The \textit{accuracy graph} (\cref{figure: best mars state accuracy}) shows a high accuracy for the MARS models, on frames where both arms hold the same position.
However, the MARS models have very poor performance on frames where the two individual arm positions are dissimilar, such as when one arm is positioned \textit{high}, and the other is positioned \textit{low}.
The \textit{likelihood graph} (\cref{figure: best mars state likelihood}) provides more insight into the cause of these results.
\textit{MARS partial standard} and \textit{standard to free} predict the \textit{pose state} where both arms are in the \textit{middle} position disproportionately often; consequently, prediction position states where the arms are in very distinct positions are predicted significantly less frequently.
This is a similar phenomenon as seen with \textit{MARS full calib}, but to a lesser extent.

The \textit{time prediction} plots also clearly depict this tendency to "jump back to the middle position" which the MARS models exhibit.
\Cref{figure: time prediction plots} shows the predictions of each model over time, as well as the ground truth. 
Here, the predictions of the particular MARS models keep rapidly jumping between "middle" and the actual position.
% This tendency to default to holding both hands in the middle position can be more clearly seen in \cref{figure: time prediction plots}, where the MARS prediction often jumps back and forth between different predictions in rapid succession.
We tried to solve this issue by applying an averaging smoothing filter with a window size of 7 (The yellow line), but even with this extra assistance, the error of the MARS systems was significantly higher than the error of the IAmMuse system.
This issue is most likely in part because the MARS system does not take into account the temporal connections in the data; it simply predicts each frame on its own.


% Mention that, even when given all the tools we could, MARS could not perform better than IAmMuse
This shows that, even if MARS is trained on a dataset with fully representative data and a similar size to that of the MARS paper, IAmMuse still outperforms it with an accuracy that is 1.5 times higher.
% Although these MARS models do produce a reasonable estimation most of the time, they still suffer from a statistical bias, and their output is to noisy be to effectively stabalised by a smoothing algorithm.
Although these MARS models do produce a reasonable estimation most of the time, they still suffer from a statistical bias.
Furthermore, their output can not be stabilised by a smoothing algorithm due to the significant frequency at which incorrect predictions are made.
Finally, these models would also be highly impractical to use in real-world scenarios if they have to be retrained with approximately an hour of training data every time they are installed in a new space.
Therefore, we conclude that we were not able to produce a MARS model with comparable accuracy to that of IAmMuse in the problem space of \textit{arm angle estimation}, even when using 25,000 frames of representative data as a training set.
